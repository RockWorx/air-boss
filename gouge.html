EVENT 01 · THE BRIEF

You run the air wing.

A carrier flight deck has a finite number of spots (44 on a Nimitz, 48 on a Ford). Every spot assigned to a dedicated tanker, fighter escort, or sensor craft is a spot not carrying strike ordnance. Your job as Air Boss: balance the air wing, allocate tanker gas across mission profiles, and turn catapult steel into maximum combat effect delivered at range.

EVENT 02 · SPOT THE DECK

Build your mix.

Carrier CCAs: four CCX-1 fit in three deck spots (0.75 spots each), stealthy and fast-turning. They can fly as strikers (less payload per spot, fewer crew at risk) or on defensive CAP (same strike wing, fewer crew in the air). Carrier landing gear and approach margins cap unrefueled clean radius (420 nm), so going deep needs tanker gas, and heavy bring-back weapons increase bolter risk unless a recovery tanker is overhead.

New in v3.4: close your own carrier CCA under Your Design — select engine class, wing area, ingress Mach, payload, and fuel, closing approach speed (C_L,app = 1.10), thrust margin, fuel tankage, and length-augmented deck spot constraints simultaneously. Catalog unit costs are rounded public or illustrative figures, not normalized to one dollar year.

Allocate spots across strike, tankers, CAP escorts, and ISR/EW. Stealth strike-fighters bring long unrefueled legs and deep survivability; workhorses turn fast on the catapults with heavy bomb loads; uncrewed CCAs offer low-cost tempo. Every airframe trades payload, unrefueled combat radius, survivability, and deck turn time.

F-35C combat radius: 600 nm, the program current estimate (equal to the threshold), from the F-35C program's published selected acquisition report; that is the value the game flies. 667 nm demonstrated is shown for reference only and is not used in the model.

EVENT 03 · STRIKE ORDNANCE

Speed vs. warhead: know your target.

Air-to-ground ordnance is a strict trade between flight speed, warhead mass, and magazine depth. The target posture dictates the weapon:

Blackbeard / MACEMach 5+ prompt · 250 lb · 120 mag
Heavy StandoffSubsonic · 1,000 lb penetrator · 36 mag
Direct AttackVolume JDAM/SDB · 500 lb · 360 mag

Time-sensitive targets: Mach 5+ Blackbeard/MACE kills fleeting coordinates before they move. Hardened bunkers: Heavy Standoff (AGM-158) cracks concrete where small warheads fail (defeat factor 1.0 vs 0.40).

EVENT 04 · TANKER TACTICS

Beyond the single curve: four profiles.

You don't just "buy gas"; you allocate tankers across four distinct tactical profiles across the cyclic day:

Yo-Yo Tanker ~18 min mission
Launches off the bow cat, tops off outbound strikers overhead to replace climb fuel, and recovers immediately with the incoming event. Minimal deck time; near-boat fuel only.
Strike Tanker Enroute push
Escorts the strike package to an enroute orbit (e.g. 150–350 nm). Bridges enroute fuel deficits beyond clean combat radius, but is committed for the entire cyclic event.
Consolidation Relay 2-ship team
Two same-type tankers launch. At 150 nm, the donor transfers fuel to the receiver and returns to recover; the receiver presses deep to hold extended orbits.
Recovery Tanker Safety net
Sits overhead during the recovery window for low-fuel returners and bolters. Prevents mission losses and diverts; cannot be spent on outbound strikes.
EVENT 05 · REACH IS THE TRADE

Why consolidate? Reach is the trade.

Why burn two tankers to push one forward? Reach. A tanker can only push as far as its fuel capacity minus round-trip burn and terminal recovery reserve allows:

F/A-18 5-Wet Single 212 nm
F/A-18 5-Wet Relay 274 nm
MQ-25 Stingray 1,900 nm

Inside single reach (≤212 nm): Consolidation loses — two singles deliver more total fuel (3,075 lb/day vs 2,050 lb at 150 nm).
Beyond single reach (>212 nm): A single tanker cannot return with reserve (infeasible!). Consolidation is the only way to pass gas at 250 nm.
MQ-25: Single reach (1,900 nm in this ledger) already spans the theater map; buddy relay is the primary tactical trade. Its give is anchored to the program's published air-refueling requirement: at least 14,000 lb of fuel given at 500 nm from the carrier (threshold; objective 16,000 lb; current estimate at least 14,000 lb). That is a requirement and an estimate, not demonstrated performance: the same published table lists demonstrated performance as TBD. Give at other stations is an illustrative game law (14,000 lb plus 10 lb for every nm closer than 500 nm).

EVENT 07 · OPERATIONAL SCENARIOS

Four operational tests: sea, inland, strait & a 3-day campaign.

Four curated missions test your air wing mix, weapon selection, and tanker doctrine:

War at Sea 150 nm Orbit
Question: Can you kill relocatable naval surface combatants before coordinates go cold?
Hint: Mach 5+ Blackbeard / MACE prompt strike + forward ISR/EW cueing + strike tankers at 150 nm.
Trap: Subsonic Heavy Standoff without ISR — long flight time lets targets move, wasting 1,000-lb warheads on empty water.
Deep Strike Inland 350 nm Orbit
Question: Can you penetrate defended airspace to crack hardened command bunkers?
Hint: Heavy Standoff (1,000 lb penetrator) + F-35C stealth + MQ-25 strike tankers holding 350 nm (buddy relay is limited to 274.2 nm).
Trap: Lightweight MACE (takes a 60% defeat penalty on hardened concrete) or unrefueled Direct Attack (shot down).
Contested Strait 500 nm · Dense Opp
Question: Can you defend the fleet against heavy peer air opposition while delivering strike effect across a 500 nm strait?
Objective: Survivability with an effect floor: fuel support × mean survivability × min(1, effect / 350), shown out of 100. Stopping the invasion force takes about 350 effect. Deliver that and your score is simply how well you protected the wing; fall short and the score shrinks in proportion (half the effect, half the score). A wing that keeps everyone safe but cannot stop the landing has not won.
Trade: The balanced preset scores 78.5 versus 57.8 for the specified strike-heavy wing, but raw effect is 458.4 versus 826.9. Wings that hide behind escorts lose: a minimalist strike wing (12 F-35C, 2 F/A-18, 2 MQ-25, 20 CAP, 8 ISR) protects better (93.4) but delivers only 224 effect and scores 59.9; a wing just short of 200 effect scores 52.8; a token escort-heavy wing (8 F-35C, 6 MQ-25, 22 CAP, 8 ISR) delivers 109 effect and scores 29.1. The floor guarantees the balanced preset beats any wing below about 275 effect; above that it is not a universal ranking proof. Illustrative, not for planning.
Contested Strait: 3 Days Ford · Surge · 500 nm
Question: Can the wing keep up the fight for three days, not just one?
Lesson: The deck is limited per recovery window; the air wing is limited by maintenance, crews and ordnance over time. Day 1 surges to about 171 launches; a day above 130% of the 120/day routine target costs the next day +2% turn time, and a two-day average above it costs 5% availability.
Trap: The 120-round prompt-strike magazine is spent on day 1 and the fallback bombs on day 2, so day 3 launches only support sorties: about 115 sorties/day on average, and strike jets sit on deck, held for weapons. Magazines, not the deck, end this campaign. Illustrative, not for planning.

EVENT 09 · READ THE DAY

Read the day: the tally & the ball.

Sorties generated per event, per aircraft type — and the day's Effect Index. Anchored to realistic carrier surge/sustained rates (~1.5–2.6 sorties/jet/day), not fantasy.

Mission-governed launch: a strike jet launches only when its own fuel plus tanker gas it can actually receive, a weapon, and a deck recovery slot support the whole sortie. The rest are held on deck and the readout names the binding resource (weapons, fuel, or deck). An unsupplied jet is never launched for zero effect.

No daily wall: recoveries are bounded per event — about 16 traps in a routine 12-minute window, 29 in a surged 22-minute window (45 s per trap) — times the events flown: 8 events sustained, 10 surged. A single surge day can pass 140 sorties. The routine rate is a target averaged over days, shown in the 3-day campaign.

Overhead: even in combat, functional check flights (about 3% of scheduled sorties) and aviator qual flights (about 2%) are flown by crewed strike fighters. They use deck slots and airframes and deliver zero effect.

New in v3.2 — tempo is a logistics bill: the daily tally also solves for fuel burned (gal/day), weapons expended (short tons/day), Combat Logistics Force resupply shuttles, aircrew fatigue limits (daily, 7-day surge, 30-day sustained), and supervisory CCA console operators.

New in v3.3 / v3.3.1 — guided tour comparison: "Watch the Air Boss" tours the air wing with adaptive pacing controls and lets you compare three CCA modes (Off crewed baseline, CAP defensive cover, or Strike mass) side by side on the same deck, measuring delivered effect, aircrew flight hours saved, and cost per combat effect.

EVENT 11 · THE LOGISTICS BILL

Tempo is a logistics bill.

Open the Logistics Pipeline & Bill view. You choose the sortie tempo; the model solves for the fuel, weapons, supply ships and people it takes to keep that tempo going. The flight deck is an engine that turns logistics and rested aircrew into combat effect.

The daily bill 115 / day
A normal 115-sortie day at 200 nm burns about 178,250 gal of JP-5 (1,550 gal a sortie) and 85.6 short tons of ordnance. Six of those sorties are check and qualification flights: no weapons, no effect.
Endurance: usable fuel lasts 12.6 days, the magazine 19.6. Surge to 180 and fuel runs out in 8.1 days. Strike at 500 nm and each sortie burns 2,382 gal, but the example cycle schedule flies only 75 sorties a day there, so the daily bill (178,646 gal) is slightly lower: fuel per sortie and sortie count both set it.
Distance sags the deck 200 → 500 nm
Aircraft do not teleport. At 200 nm a 14-hour day fits 8 events, 120 sorties. Push the strike out to 500 nm and each sortie takes 3.07 hours: under this example cycle schedule, 5 events, 75 sorties.
Trap: this is one scheduling policy, not the deck’s hard limit. Real air wings can double-cycle long sorties (launch in one event, recover two events later) instead of stretching every event.
The supply shuttle 750 → 1,800 nm
One fleet oiler and one ammunition ship keep a 180-sortie surge fed when the logistics hub is 750 nm away (6.97-day round trip). At that tempo usable fuel lasts only 8.1 days, so at 1,200 nm (a 9.06-day round trip) a second oiler must be staggered in: 3 ships. At 1,800 nm (11.83 days) the pipeline doubles to 4 ships.
Trap: the shuttle is sized as if every delivery arrives on time. Alongside fueling and highline cargo can continue with flight operations when wind and sea state permit, but helicopter replenishment cannot, and weather can delay a delivery.
Misses are part of the tail 50 targets
Guided bombs hit about 72% of the time. Salvo doctrine (two per target) defeats 46 of 50 targets with 100 weapons and 50 sorties. Shoot-look-shoot defeats the same 46 with 64 weapons and 32 sorties, but needs a second wave of 7 re-attack sorties.
Beast mode: external pylons triple the bombs per stealth-fighter sortie, but burn 20% more fuel and cut survival to release from 0.95 to 0.78. It is a day-level choice: a 12-hour deck reset to enter, debited against the fly day (the return to Stealth is not charged in this model).
People run out first 62 ready pilots
44 fighters with 65 pilots, 62 ready after med-downs. Public flight-time guidance allows about 30 hours in 7 days and 65 in 30. On 2.5-hour sorties that caps the wing near 106 sorties a day for a week and 54 a day over a month. Sustainable means under every limit: over a month, the 30-day limit binds first.
CCAs still need people: with an airborne quarterback, one ship operator supervises 4 CCAs (the default control ratio, a teaching assumption you can change from 1 to 8), so 12 CCAs need 3 consoles and 5 operators on watch at each launch and recovery and none en route. Covering a 14-hour flight day takes 2 shifts (10 assigned); an 18- to 24-hour surge day takes 3 (15). At 2 CCAs per operator that is 9 on watch (18 / 27); at 8, 3 on watch (6 / 9). Controlling every CCA from the ship takes one console per CCA: 36 to 54. Illustrative, not for planning.
EVENT 12 · YOUR DESIGN

An unclosed airplane flies only in PowerPoint.

Open Your Design in the roster. You do not buy features with points; you pick an engine class, a wing area, an ingress Mach, payload and fuel, and the airplane exists only if four gates close at once: carrier approach speed, ingress thrust margin, fuel volume and deck footprint. A design that does not close stays on deck and delivers zero effect.

The default closes Mid-thrust, 285 sq ft
A mid-thrust dry turbofan, a 285-sq-ft wing, Mach 0.80, 2,000 lb of payload and 5,620 lb of fuel: approach 107.5 kt against the 135-kt limit, D/T 0.48 against 0.85, tanks exactly full, 0.57 deck spots (39.8 ft long, 25.5 ft folded). Breguet gives about 1,005 nm of clean radius. Unit recurring flyaway $18.94M; APUC-style procurement cost (with support and spares) $21.78M; with $600M of development spread over 150 aircraft, the PAUC-style unit cost is $25.78M.
Thrust-sensitivity check: knock installed thrust down 12% and D/T is still 0.548, so it is a robust closure, not a marginal one. A deterministic check, not a hot-day model.
The carrier pinch light engine, M0.82
A small wing lands too fast; a big wing drags too much. Give a light engine 4,000 lb of payload and 4,500 lb of fuel at Mach 0.82: it needs about 178 sq ft just to land at 135 kt, but no wing from 150 to 450 sq ft gets D/T below 0.93. Pinched out: build drag term by term and the window can vanish.
Trap: never borrow an L/D from another shape. Wetted area, lift-induced drag and wave drag come from this airframe.
Keep up, or pay for it Mach 0.65 at 500 nm
The strike package pushes at Mach 0.80. A Mach 0.65 CCA must launch 14.7 minutes early. In the first event of the day there is no earlier window, so 12 fighters would have to hold at the push point: 13,210 lb of loiter gas, 1 MQ-25 sortie (17,500 lb at the 150-nm station). With no spare tanker sortie the CCA is withheld from that event; the fighters push on time and the CCA joins at the next event.
Other options: let the fighters loiter every event (tanker gas each time) or slow the whole package (no gas, longer exposure).
Price versus value engine NRE
The engine is priced as its own hardware, and its development is spread over the buy: a heavy core adds $12.0M per aircraft at a buy of 50 but $2.0M at 300. Cost per combat effect divides program unit cost by the effect the wing actually delivers; an unclosed design delivers none, so its cost per effect is infinite.
Watch the Air Boss (v3.3.1): the guided tour holds each card for a reading time scaled to its words: at least 6 seconds on Relaxed, 3.6 on Normal, 2.1 on Brisk (longer for longer explanations), with Pause, Next, Space, Right Arrow, and the pace control. Compare three force-mix modes on the same deck: Off (crewed baseline), CAP (same strike wing, fewer crew in the air), or Strike (less payload per spot, fewer crew at risk).
